Half Moon · Paradise · October–March

Gokarna Beach Camping

Two beaches in Gokarna still let you fall asleep to the surf: Half Moon and Paradise. No streetlights, no roads and patchy signal — just seasonal tent camps on the sand.

Season: October–March Tents: ₹500–₹1,000 Walk-in or boat-in only

Overview

Camping in Gokarna, honestly

Gokarna is not a resort camping destination. The two camping beaches — Half Moon and Paradise — have no road access, no permanent campsites and no facilities beyond what seasonal operators bring in for the winter. That is exactly why people love it.

From October to March, local operators set up tent camps on the sand: basic dome tents, mattresses, a shared bonfire and simple meals. You can also bring your own tent if you are self-sufficient and carry everything out with you. Camping is not permitted in the temple town area, and the far cove of Nirvana has nothing at all — treat it as wild coastline.

Book in peak weeks. Between Christmas and New Year, and around long weekends, tents at Half Moon and Paradise fill quickly. Reserve through your homestay or a local operator before you walk in.

Camping at a glance

  • Where: Half Moon and Paradise beaches
  • Season: October–March
  • Tent rental: ₹500–₹1,000 per night
  • Access: trek or boat only
  • Facilities: basic, seasonal
  • Signal: weak to none
  • Cash: essential

Half Moon Beach camping

The quieter of the two camps, a small crescent 20–45 minutes on foot from Om or a short boat ride. Half Moon has seasonal shacks in peak weeks, which makes it the easier first camping experience: you can still get a cooked meal and a bottle of water without carrying everything in.

Good for: first-time beach campers, couples, small groups. Prefer a bed? Explore Half Moon secluded cottages.

Paradise Beach camping

Wilder and more remote, reached by a boulder traverse from Half Moon or a boat from Om. Paradise is where the night sky gets properly dark and the water sometimes glows with bioluminescence. Facilities are minimal, so bring what you need and expect to carry it out.

Good for: stargazers, seasoned campers, unplugging completely. Prefer eco pods? Explore Paradise Beach eco cliff pods.

Practicalities

What it costs and what to bring

What to bring

  • Head torch or lantern — there is no lighting on the beach
  • 2–3 litres of water per person; refills are limited
  • Snacks, electrolytes and any food you cannot buy at a seasonal shack
  • Cash in small notes
  • Power bank, dry bag and a light rain shell
  • Insect repellent and a basic first-aid kit
  • A warm layer — coastal nights can feel cool after the sun drops

Safety and leave no trace

  • Camp with others and keep valuables close; there is no security.
  • Do not swim at night, and never swim alone in these coves.
  • Secure tents before dark; coastal wind picks up quickly.
  • Fires only where the operator manages them; never leave embers.
  • Carry all rubbish back to town — including bottles and wrappers.
  • No medical facilities on the beaches; know your evacuation route by boat.

FAQs

Gokarna camping questions

Where can you camp in Gokarna?

Half Moon and Paradise are the two camping beaches, with seasonal tent camps from October to March. Both are reachable only on foot or by boat. There are no permanent campsites, and camping is not allowed in the temple town area.

How much does camping in Gokarna cost?

Rented tents on Half Moon and Paradise typically cost ₹500–₹1,000 per night depending on the season and whether meals are included. Bring cash, as there are no ATMs or reliable card payments on the beaches.

Is camping in Gokarna safe?

Camping is generally safe in season with basic precautions. There are no lifeguards or medical facilities, phone signal is patchy, and the sea is strong. Camp with others, secure valuables, avoid swimming at night and never camp during the monsoon.

When is the camping season in Gokarna?

October to March. November to February offers the calmest seas and clearest skies. Camps close during the monsoon months of June to September.
